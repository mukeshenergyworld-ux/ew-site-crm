EW CRM

The whole book — clients, quotes, sites, hisab, service, incentives

Who this is for: everyone in the office. The CRM is the whole book — clients, quotes, sites, hisab, service, incentives, stock. What you see depends on your role, and a man may hold more than one.

Signing in

Your name and Your PIN, then Sign in. Every app in the estate uses this same name and PIN. There is only ever one.

Your PIN is yours. Nobody else should ever type it, and nobody should ever ask you for it.

Roles — who sees what

The role is a tick box in the team sheet, set by the owner. Tick more than one and the man gets every screen either role opens.

RoleWhat it opens
adminEverything.
accountsClients, challans, payments, HISAB, service, spares, dues, stock, rates and the price list.
godownToday, material returns, tools, challans, products, stock. Nothing about money.
salesHis clients, quotes, leads, sites, follow-ups, challans, payments and his own monthly card.
serviceToday, service, spares, dues, follow-ups, products.

Looking a man up — everything about him at once

  1. Open Search.
  2. Type his name, his firm, his area or his number. Two letters is enough.
  3. Tap him in the results.
  4. You get the lot on one screen: his sites, his challans, his quotes, what he owes and what he has paid — broken down month by month, not in one lump.

If the same man was entered twice under two spellings, the two are shown as one once they have been merged. See below.

Three screens that catch mistakes

  • Duplicate check — the same man entered twice. It finds exact repeats and spelling mistakes: two names the same length where one word differs by a single letter, which is how “Mahavir” and “Mahabir” both ended up on the book. Merging keeps both names — the second becomes another way of saying the first, and his money is added together.
  • Health check — rows with something missing that will bite later.
  • Pending upload — anything still sitting on somebody’s phone.

Registering an installation or an AMC

Only a lead marked Won can get a service record — a won quote, a won pitch, or a delivered challan. Win it first.

On the form, set the install date and the AMC. The AMC has three choices and they decide what the customer is charged:

  • AMC with spares — nothing is charged at all.
  • AMC without spares — the visit and the salt are free, the parts are billed.
  • None — everything is charged.
If a contract already carries an older word — Gold, Yearly, Visit-based — that word is kept when you open the form, so looking at a signed contract never silently re-words it. But the engineer’s app has to guess what it covers. Set it to one of the three and the guessing stops.

Rules that hold everywhere

  • Nothing is ever deleted. Block it, mark it inactive, reverse it with a counter-entry. There is no delete anywhere in this estate and there is not going to be one.
  • Every PIN is typed by the man it belongs to. Not shared, not written down, not reset by anyone but the owner.
  • The word is incentive. On a screen, in a message, in a conversation with a partner — it is never called anything else.
  • If the app cannot work something out, it says so. It does not show a zero and hope. A blank where a number should be means the app does not know — that is information, not a fault.

Keeping the app up to date

The number beside each app on the launcher page is what the server is serving. Open the app and look at its own header. If the two do not match, that phone is running an old copy — tap the ↻ arrow inside the app and it will offer the update.

The icon on a phone never updates by itself. If an app is on a home screen with the old picture: press and hold it, remove it, and add it again from the launcher. You stay signed in.
